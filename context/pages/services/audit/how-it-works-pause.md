## How it works

- **Pause** (`src/Scout.Audit/RepositoryAudits.cs:190`): `pause_repository_audit` asks for it, only when the user asks to pause. A running job is paused with the reason `you paused it` (`PausedByOwner`, `:76`), whether a batch is in progress or not, and the owner reads `Paused the audit of owner/repo; an area in progress finishes first. The findings so far stay on the cards. <progress> Ask Scout to continue the audit of owner/repo to resume it from the first area not done.`, true whether an area is in progress, the worker is between areas, or the job waits behind another repository's; the worker finishes the batch in progress, keeps and posts its findings, posts the owner's pause notice, and runs no further batch (`tests/Scout.Audit.Tests/AuditWorkerTests.cs:710`); a pause during the last batch leaves nothing pending, and continuing ends the job with its last post (`:756`). Each owner action while the audit runs, and the posts that follow it, each once and to the conversation the job holds: `tests/Scout.Audit.Tests/AuditWorkerTests.cs:604`. A job already paused, by the owner or by the AI Source, is left as it is and the owner reads `The audit of owner/repo is already paused: <reason>. <progress> Ask Scout to continue …`; a job the AI Source paused between the read and the write is answered the same way, because the store pauses only a running job (`tests/Scout.Audit.Tests/RepositoryAuditsTests.cs:407`). With no job, or a stopped or done one, the answer is `No audit of owner/repo is running.` A start resumes a job the owner paused exactly as one the AI Source paused, from the first batch not done. The pause writes the job's status row in `audit.db` and nothing else, and a refused write is the store's sentence, the job still running (`tests/Scout.Audit.Tests/RepositoryAuditsTests.cs:425`). The table of every action on every state, with the sentence, the row after and whether the worker is woken, is `tests/Scout.Audit.Tests/RepositoryAuditsTests.cs:236`.

- **Continue from Scout Desktop** (`src/Scout.Audit/RepositoryAudits.cs:187`, through `ControlAsync`, `:196`, and `:219`, 2026-11-10): the owner's Continue click reaches the job through the worker's control poller ([[pages/services/host-console]]), with no conversation of its own, so `origin()` is null and the job keeps the conversation it was started from (`:276`). It resumes only a paused job, from its first area not done, with the resume sentence; a running job answers `The audit of owner/repo is already running. <progress>`; none, a stopped or a done job answers `No paused audit of owner/repo to continue.` It never takes a snapshot nor starts an audit, which a start would do for a stopped or done job. `ControlAsync` applies the desktop's pause, continue or stop and says whether the click changed the job (`Applied`): true only for a running job paused, a paused job continued and a running or paused job stopped; the desktop's record of the click carries it as its status ([[pages/services/control-plane]]). The desktop's pause and stop go through `PauseAsync` and `StopAsync` as the chat's do, so their notices, the owner's pause notice and the stop notice, go to the job's conversation the same way (`tests/Scout.Audit.Tests/AuditWorkerTests.cs:662`); the table of every desktop control on every state, with the sentence, whether it changed the job, the row and the conversation after, is `tests/Scout.Audit.Tests/RepositoryAuditsTests.cs:307`.

- **Stop and status** (`src/Scout.Audit/RepositoryAudits.cs:193`, `:253`): stop writes the job's status row and nothing else, for a running or a paused job, and the owner reads `Stopped the audit of owner/repo; an area in progress finishes first. The findings so far stay on the cards. <progress>`; the status is counted from the rows: areas done, not covered, findings, files read, and a paused job names its reason: `The audit of owner/repo at <commit> is paused: <reason>.`
