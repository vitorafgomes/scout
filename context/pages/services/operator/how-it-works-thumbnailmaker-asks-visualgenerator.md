## How it works

`ThumbnailMaker` (`src/Scout.Operator/ThumbnailMaker.cs:12`) asks `VisualGenerator.ThumbnailAsync` (`src/Scout.Operator/VisualGenerator.cs:114`) for a picture of the first title, with the image settings and a prompt that forbids text, logos, trademarks and real people, counted against the same 40 pictures a day, and writes the title it was made for to `thumbnail.txt` beside it (`src/Scout.Operator/ThumbnailMaker.cs:16`); a later assembly whose title is the same uses that picture again without asking the image service (`:29`); a title file that cannot be read or written only means the picture is made again next time. Without an image service, with the day's pictures spent, or when the service fails or returns no bytes, which gives the place back, it removes `thumbnail.txt` and saves the frame at a tenth of the video instead (`:48`, `:50`), taken again on every assembly. Either way the file is `thumbnail.png`. The thumbnail of an `own_video` project is always that frame, and the image service is never asked (`src/Scout.Operator/ThumbnailMaker.cs:34`, `tests/Scout.Operator.Tests/ThumbnailMakerTests.cs:148`). The maker also returns what the picture shows, the package's `ThumbnailDescription`: for a generated or reused picture what was asked of it, never the prompt, "A picture made for the video \"<title>\": one bold, simple subject.", beside `ThumbnailPrompt` (`src/Scout.Operator/VisualGenerator.cs:165`); for the frame "A frame from the video at a tenth of its length." (`src/Scout.Operator/ThumbnailMaker.cs:20`; `tests/Scout.Operator.Tests/ThumbnailMakerTests.cs:32`, `:52`, `:100`).

`PackageChecks` (`src/Scout.Operator/PackageChecks.cs:14`) builds the first metadata and six checks. The title is the first title variant, else the topic, cut to 100 characters at the last word that fits, with no trailing space (`:137`, `:175`); the description is the script's description followed by a chapters list and a `Credits` block, `0:00 <heading>` lines timed from the measured scene lengths: each section starts at its first scene, the first is forced to 0:00, a chapter less than 10 s after the previous one or before the end is dropped, and the list is left out unless three remain, YouTube's rule (`:148`, `:256`); the `Credits` block is one line per distinct credit of the scenes whose visual source is `stock`, in scene order (`:152`, `tests/Scout.Operator.Tests/PackageChecksTests.cs:236`); a description over 5000 characters cuts the script's text at a word and keeps the chapters list and the credits whole, or, when they alone do not fit, is cut as a whole (`tests/Scout.Operator.Tests/PackageChecksTests.cs:246`, `src/Scout.Operator/PackageChecks.cs:169`). The tags are the script's, cleaned, at most 100 characters each, kept in order while they fit 500 characters counted YouTube's way, two more for a tag with a space and a comma between tags (`:201`, `:216`). Nothing self-promotional is added. When the owner edited the metadata, the title, description and tags go back as read (`src/Scout.Operator/AssemblyStage.cs:125`), and so do `rights_confirmed`, `approved_at` and `metadata_edited`, `metadata_saved_at` and `rights_confirmed_at`, always. `AssembledAt` is the render's end on the worker's `TimeProvider` (`src/Scout.Operator/AssemblyStage.cs:69`, `tests/Scout.Operator.Tests/AssemblyTests.cs:184`); a failed package has neither it nor a description. The checks (`src/Scout.Operator/PackageChecks.cs:62` to `:133`): `duration` fails under 0:30 or over 12 hours; `loudness` warns outside -18 to -11 LUFS integrated, measured with `-af loudnorm=print_format=json -f null -` and read from the `input_i` of the JSON at the end of the error output (`:220`), and warns when it cannot be measured; `captions` fails when a scene has narration and no caption line; `placeholders` fails when a scene is not ready or misses its picture or narration file, a scene shown over the project's background counting as having its picture (`tests/Scout.Operator.Tests/PackageChecksTests.cs:77`); `metadata` fails on a title outside 1 to 100 characters, a description over 5000, or tags over 500; `discoverability` warns when the title shares no word of three letters or more with the topic or the mandate's pillars, or the description has no `0:00` chapter line, and passes otherwise.

`FfmpegToolset.ProbeAsync` (`src/Scout.Operator/FfmpegToolset.cs:108`) keeps a probe that found the tools for the life of the process, and answers a failed one again for 2 minutes, then probes again (`:77`, `:113`), so an ffmpeg installed after the worker started is found without a restart. `ffmpeg` from `Scout:Operator:FfmpegPath` must answer `-hide_banner -version`; with the setting empty it is looked for as `ffmpeg` on the system path, then `/opt/homebrew/bin/ffmpeg`, then `/usr/local/bin/ffmpeg` (`:79`), because an app started from the macOS Finder does not get the shell's path; a configured path that fails is never replaced. There is no `ffprobe`. The font file at `FontPath` must exist; `ffmpeg -hide_banner -encoders` must list a usable encoder pair, picked in the order above (`:158`); `-filters` must list every filter the assembly uses, `anullsrc`, `apad`, `aresample`, `chromakey`, `color`, `crop`, `drawtext`, `fade`, `format`, `gblur`, `loudnorm`, `overlay`, `pad`, `scale`, `setsar`, `split` and `zoompan` (`:81`, `:181`; the bundled LGPL build lists both `fade` and `zoompan`, and an ffmpeg without either is refused, `tests/Scout.Operator.Tests/FfmpegToolsetTests.cs:125`), and `-demuxers` the `concat` and `lavfi` demuxers (`src/Scout.Operator/FfmpegToolset.cs:84`, `:193`). Otherwise assembly is unavailable with one of "Scout could not run ffmpeg, so videos cannot be assembled.", "Scout has no font for captions and text cards, so videos cannot be assembled.", "The ffmpeg Scout found has no H.264 or VP9 encoder it can use, so videos cannot be assembled." or "The ffmpeg Scout found has no <name> filter, so videos cannot be assembled." (or `demuxer`); when it is available the note is "Videos are assembled with ffmpeg and the <encoder> encoder."

`ProcessRunner` (`src/Scout.Operator/ProcessRunner.cs:34`) starts the program with `UseShellExecute` off and each argument added to `ArgumentList` (`:58`), redirects standard input (`:51`) and writes to it only the input the caller gives, as UTF-8 with no byte order mark, then closes it, or closes it at once without one (`:73`, `:118`), because the worker's own standard input is the quick-ask channel and the watchdog's end-of-input signal ([[pages/services/host-console]]); the input is written beside the run, so a program that exits without reading it still returns its exit code and error output, and a program that stops reading still meets its timeout. It reads standard output whole as bytes and decodes it to text, or, when the caller asks for `rawOutput`, hands back only the bytes with the text empty, which only `FaceMouth` asks for, for the presenter picture ffmpeg decodes, so no other run holds a second copy or a string it does not read (`src/Scout.Operator/ProcessRunner.cs:89`, `tests/Scout.Operator.Tests/ProcessRunnerTests.cs:115`, `:19`), and keeps the last 8192 characters of standard error (`src/Scout.Operator/ProcessRunner.cs:36`), and kills the program and every process it started on a timeout or a cancellation (`:105`). A program that cannot be started fails with "Scout could not start <name>."; one past its timeout with "<name> ran longer than <n> seconds and was stopped."

`ScenePlanner` (`src/Scout.Operator/ScenePlanner.cs:18`) makes one scene of the hook, one per section, and one of the call to action, in that order; a section whose narration is longer than 700 characters is split at sentence ends into parts of at most 700 characters, a single sentence longer than that staying whole (`:266`). It asks the model once, through `ModelJson`, for exactly one picture prompt per scene in the mandate's voice and style, followed, when the project carries the owner's direction, by "Direction the owner gave for this video: <direction>" and the instruction to write every prompt and every search term in keeping with it, nothing when the direction is empty (`:296`, `:303`, `tests/Scout.Operator.Tests/ScenePlannerTests.cs:87`, `tests/Scout.Operator.Tests/ProductionTests.cs:71`), never a real person's likeness, a public figure, a logo, a trademark or readable text, and in the same request, as `search_terms`, one entry per scene of 2 to 4 plain English nouns or adjectives without quotes that find a photograph of the scene in a library of freely licensed pictures; a list of prompts of the wrong length is asked for once more, and a second one fails the project with "The AI model did not return a picture prompt for every scene in the expected form, twice." The terms are optional: a scene whose entry is missing or blank gets an empty `VisualQuery`, and the quotes, control characters and repeated spaces of the others are removed (`src/Scout.Operator/ScenePlanner.cs:74`, `tests/Scout.Operator.Tests/ScenePlannerTests.cs:73`). Scenes are `s1`, `s2`, ... in order, `planned`, with audio `none` and a `text_card` visual. Nothing caps the number of scenes; the picture cap below does.

`NarrationGenerator` (`src/Scout.Operator/NarrationGenerator.cs:20`) makes the narration in the project's `narration_voice` (`:41`), into the file stem the caller names, `scene-<id>` unless it is the presenter preview (`:37`). A `piper:` id is a bundled voice, made by `PiperSpeech` and saved as `scene-<id>.wav` (`:47`, `:80`); `<Provider>:<voice>` is a voice of the connected provider, when the provider is the one connected and the voice is one of its `MediaCapabilities.SpeechVoices`, sent to the kernel's `ITextToAudioService` with `MediaCapabilities.SpeechSettingsFor(voice)` and saved as `scene-<id>.mp3` (`:65`, `:104`). With no voice chosen, the provider's speech service in its default voice with `MediaCapabilities.SpeechSettings` (`:70`), else the default bundled voice (`:73`), else audio `none` and the note "No narration: the connected AI Source has no speech service in Scout; attach your own audio in Scout Desktop, Production." Any other id, a provider that is not the connected one, a voice it does not offer or a bundled voice not on this machine, fails the scene with "The narration voice '<id>' is not available on this machine; choose another in Scout Desktop, Production." (`:62`) before anything runs. The duration is always the estimate at 150 words a minute (`:30`), because no engine reports the audio's length. A provider service that throws, or returns no bytes, fails the scene with "The AI Source could not make the narration of scene <n>; regenerate it in Scout Desktop, Production."; the WAV is read by name like the MP3 in assembly, since `MediaProbe` and `Assembler` take the file the scene names and ffmpeg reads both.
