## How it works

- **Model not found:** an `LlmModelNotFoundException`, the AI Source's HTTP 404 for the model Scout asked for ([[pages/services/llm]]), on Ollama a local model not installed yet or still downloading, completes the reply with `StreamPumpOptions.ModelNotFoundMessage` formatted with the model id, "The AI Source does not have the model <id>. A local model is not installed yet or is still downloading: check AI Sources in Scout Desktop." (`src/Scout.Conversation/Streaming/StreamPumpOptions.cs:106`, caught at `src/Scout.Conversation/Streaming/StreamPump.cs:185`), worded to be true for any provider, with no word of error or failure, instead of the generic mid-flight failure; it is logged as one Warning naming the model, without the exception (`tests/Scout.Conversation.Tests/Streaming/StreamPumpFailureTests.cs:195`, `When_The_Provider_Does_Not_Have_The_Model_The_Reply_Names_It_And_Says_Where_To_Look_Not_An_Error`, and the `model not found` row of the two error-ending tables). The chat is not retried when the download ends.

- **Quota or usage limit:** an `LlmQuotaExceededException` completes the reply with `StreamPumpOptions.QuotaExceededMessage`, except that the code `subscription_sharing_usage_limit_exceeded`, the ChatGPT plan's usage limit for apps, completes it with `StreamPumpOptions.PlanUsageLimitMessage`, "The ChatGPT plan reached its usage limit for apps like Scout; try again later, or choose another AI Source in Scout Desktop." (`src/Scout.Conversation/Streaming/StreamPumpOptions.cs:77`, chosen at `src/Scout.Conversation/Streaming/StreamPump.cs:147`; `tests/Scout.Llm.Tests/ChatGatewayEmptyReplyLogTests.cs:193`), whether the plan refused with an HTTP 429 or ended a stream with that code ([[pages/services/llm]]), and the code `credit_balance_too_low`, the Claude API account out of credit, completes it with `StreamPumpOptions.CreditExhaustedMessage`, "The AI provider's account has run out of credit. Add credit with the provider, or choose another AI Source in Scout Desktop." (`src/Scout.Conversation/Streaming/StreamPumpOptions.cs:70`), a plain statement with no word of error, rejection or failure, at the owner's request; every one logs the same Warning naming the code, without a stack (`tests/Scout.Conversation.Tests/Streaming/StreamPumpFailureTests.cs`, `When_The_Claude_Api_Account_Has_No_Credit_Left_The_Reply_Says_The_Balance_Ran_Out_Not_An_Error`, and the credit row of the error-ending tables).

- **A picture on the message** ([[pages/decisions/0035-screenshot-attached-to-a-question]]): the router copies `ChatMessage.Image` to `ChatRequest.Image` as a `ChatImage` of the same bytes and MIME type (`src/Scout.Conversation/Routing/LlmStreamingRouter.cs:115`), so no Semantic Kernel type enters this project; memory keeps the turn's text only, never the picture. The voice rewrite keeps the image with the transcript (`src/Scout.Conversation/ConversationConductor.cs:157`). When the provider cannot read images the gateway throws `LlmVisionUnavailableException` before any call, and the pump completes the reply with `StreamPumpOptions.VisionUnavailableMessage`, "The connected AI source cannot look at pictures; ask it in words or connect one that can." (`src/Scout.Conversation/Streaming/StreamPumpOptions.cs:98`, caught at `src/Scout.Conversation/Streaming/StreamPump.cs:178`), instead of answering as if no picture had been sent. `ChatMessage`'s `ToString` prints `Image = <n> bytes` or `null`, never the bytes (`src/Scout.Conversation/Abstractions/ChatMessage.cs:81`).

- **Error endings within the length limit** (2026-11-10): every reply that ends on an error is rendered within the limit the sink declared, the smaller of `RenderCapabilities.MaxBodyLength` and `StreamPumpOptions.MaxBodyLength`, and a body of exactly that length is within it. A rate-limit, quota, plan-usage, sign-in, key or model-not-found ending is the partial text, a blank line and the sentence (`WithSentence`, `src/Scout.Conversation/Streaming/StreamPump.cs:296`): the sentence is kept whole and the partial text gives way, cut from its start with the usual tail-preserving marker to the room the sentence and the blank line leave, kept whole when it fits that room exactly, or dropped when it does not fit and that room cannot hold more than the marker; the sentence itself is cut only when the limit is smaller than the sentence. The vision ending is the sentence alone, under the same cap. A failed or timed-out stream hands `FailAsync` the first part of the partial text that fits the limit, ending in `... (truncated)` when it was cut (`FirstPart`, `:281`, called at `:203`). The first part and not the newest, because the sink then adds its own failure footer and Slack and Teams cut the text again from its end to make room for it: their cut takes the pump's mark with it, so a failed reply over the limit shows its first part, one marker and the footer, as it did when the sinks cut the whole text themselves ([[pages/services/adapter-slack]], [[pages/services/adapter-teams]]). A sink that declares no limit gets the text uncut, which is Discord's case: it declares `int.MaxValue` and keeps five parts of 3,968 characters, so a partial text that fills them still ends in "(reply truncated)" without the error sentence ([[pages/roadmap/followups]]). Tests: `tests/Scout.Conversation.Tests/Streaming/StreamPumpFailureTests.cs:335` (ten endings over an empty, a short, an at-the-limit and an over-the-limit partial text, with the exact body where the partial text needs no cut), `:356`, `:371`, `:391`, `:403`, `:416`; through the real sinks, `tests/Scout.Adapter.Slack.Tests/Streaming/SlackSinkBoundaryTests.cs:263` and `tests/Scout.Adapter.Teams.Tests/Streaming/TeamsSinkStreamPumpTests.cs:111`.

- **The overall timeout and the terminal write** (2026-11-10): the pump's timeout, the smaller of `StreamPumpOptions.OverallTimeout` (`src/Scout.Conversation/Streaming/StreamPumpOptions.cs:36`) and the sink's `MaxResponseDuration`, cancels the token that `BeginAsync`, the stream, each update and the completing write run under; those end when the sink honours that token. The terminal write of a run that ended on an error is bounded whatever the sink does (`WriteTerminalAsync`, `src/Scout.Conversation/Streaming/StreamPump.cs:237`): while the timeout has not fired the write has what remains of it; once it has fired, which is how a timed-out stream reaches `FailAsync` and how a provider error caught late reaches `CompleteAsync`, the write has `StreamPumpOptions.TerminalWriteGrace`, 10 seconds by default, on the injected clock and linked to the caller (`src/Scout.Conversation/Streaming/StreamPumpOptions.cs:43`, `src/Scout.Conversation/Streaming/StreamPump.cs:243`). The pump stops waiting at that bound even when the sink ignores its token (`:254`): the write is left to finish on its own, and a failure it ends in later is observed, never raised as an unobserved task exception (`:268`). So a run that ended on an error returns at most `TerminalWriteGrace` after its timeout. The caller's cancellation still stops the run and propagates, and a caller that had already cancelled when the stream failed gets its cancellation with no terminal call at all (`:242`), the same outcome as a cancellation mid-stream: the conductor marks the turn cancelled and rethrows, and nothing is written to the chat for a turn that was stopped. A terminal write the sink rejects, by throwing or by returning a failed task, or does not finish in time is logged once, as a Warning that carries "timed out" or the exception's type name and never its message, the body or the exception, and the run returns normally: it does not hang and does not throw into the conductor. The pump refuses to be built with a negative or infinite `TerminalWriteGrace` (`:40`). The completing write of a stream that ended well is not caught by the pump: its failure or timeout reaches the conductor, which reports it through `FailAsync` on a token without a bound (`src/Scout.Conversation/ConversationConductor.cs:185`). The registered pump sets no `OverallTimeout` (`src/Scout.Conversation/ConversationServiceCollectionExtensions.cs:40`), so in the composed worker only a Teams reply, whose sink declares 120 seconds, has a ceiling, and on Discord, Slack and the desktop an error ending the sink never finishes is still stopped only by the caller ([[pages/roadmap/followups]]). Tests, on a fake clock with continuations run inline, so none waits on real time: `tests/Scout.Conversation.Tests/Streaming/StreamPumpFailureTests.cs:443` (each of the ten endings with a sink that never finishes, honouring its token or ignoring it), `:474` (a write that starts after the timeout, through `FailAsync` and through `CompleteAsync`), `:507` (the caller's cancellation before the timeout and during the grace), `:529` (a caller already cancelled), `:543` (the abandoned write's late failure), `:574` (the refused grace), `:600` and `:584` (a write the transport rejects).
