ADR 0001 — License: MIT

Scout is released under the MIT license to align with the .NET open-source ecosystem and keep adoption permissive and low-friction.

adr statusaccepted version 1.0 date 2026-04-21 sources vault 06-decisions/2026-04-21-license-mit.md; LICENSE

Status

  • Accepted — 2026-04-21. The full text lives in the source repository at LICENSE.

Context

Scout is an open-source project, so the license is a foundational decision. It shapes three downstream concerns:

  • Dependency compatibility — which libraries Scout can adopt without a license clash.
  • Consumer freedom — what users may do with Scout: redistribution, commercial use, embedding.
  • Contributor expectations — how patents and attribution are handled for incoming work.

For a .NET OSS project of this shape the realistic candidates are MIT and Apache-2.0. Both are OSI-approved, permissive, and GPL-compatible.

Decision

Scout ships under the MIT license. The committed LICENSE file carries the notice Copyright (c) 2026 Chiacho Gomes and the standard MIT permission and warranty-disclaimer text.

What MIT permits

Users may, free of charge, deal in the software without restriction, subject only to preserving the copyright and permission notice.

  • Use, copy, and modify the software
  • Merge, publish, and distribute it
  • Sublicense and sell copies
  • Embed Scout and relicense derivatives, including for commercial use
The single obligation

The copyright notice and the permission notice must be included in all copies or substantial portions of the software. The software is provided "as is", without warranty of any kind; the authors and copyright holders are not liable for any claim or damages arising from its use.

Rationale

  • Ecosystem alignment. Scout's core dependency, Microsoft Semantic Kernel, is MIT. The wider .NET OSS ecosystem (dotnet/runtime, dotnet/aspnetcore, dotnet/efcore, most community libraries) defaults to MIT.
  • Simplicity. MIT is short, well-understood, and rarely triggers legal review inside companies, so contributors from corporate environments can clear it quickly.
  • Permissive. Users can embed Scout, relicense derivatives, and use it commercially without obligations beyond preserving the copyright notice.

Alternatives

Rejected alternatives
  • Apache-2.0 — adds an explicit patent grant that matters for company-backed projects holding software patents. Scout is an individual-initiated OSS project, so the patent clause adds weight without a matching benefit. MIT grants no explicit patent rights, but this has not been a practical issue for comparable .NET projects.
  • AGPL-3.0 / GPL-3.0 — copyleft would deter corporate adoption and add friction to embedding Scout in internal tooling, the primary deployment shape, and would clash with several dependencies.
  • Dual-license (MIT + commercial) — misaligned with Scout's positioning as a community-driven OSS tool.

Consequences

Better

  • License-compatible with Semantic Kernel and the mainstream .NET OSS dependency set, so adopting new libraries rarely raises a licensing question.
  • Low legal friction for corporate contributors and consumers; no CLA is required, and the PR template carries a short DCO-style confirmation.
  • All first-party plugins and adapters inherit MIT unless they depend on incompatibly-licensed components (none planned).

Worse

  • No explicit patent grant, unlike Apache-2.0. Accepted as a low practical risk for an individual-initiated project, but it is a deliberate trade-off rather than a non-issue.
  • Permissiveness means derivatives may be closed-source; the project gives up any copyleft guarantee of contributions flowing back upstream.

Enforcement

  • LICENSE, CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md, SECURITY.md, and CONTRIBUTING.md live in the source repository.
  • Contributions are accepted under MIT; the PR template includes a short DCO-style confirmation in place of a CLA.
  • The license badge in the source README.md links to LICENSE.
  • The commercial relicensing of FluentAssertions is the reason tests use Shouldly, which stays MIT — a concrete instance of keeping the dependency tree MIT-aligned.
